01

Executive Summary

Overview

Today, PayChain earns almost entirely from one thing: a markup layered onto the real NCBA/Safaricom cost of money leaving a merchant's account. Money coming in stays free. That's a genuinely merchant-friendly design worth keeping — but it's also thin, and it caps PayChain's revenue at a small slice of the value it actually creates: real-time visibility into every merchant's cash flow, the same data a bank would spend months underwriting for.

This report proposes four revenue pillars, layered on top of the current model rather than replacing it: priced-right transfers (fixed and already live), embedded working capital, shared float yield, and a Growth tier for scaling merchants. Each is designed so PayChain earns more only when the merchant is better off, better served, or growing — never by extracting more from a transaction that hasn't changed.

02

Where PayChain Stands Today

Diagnosis

PayChain's ledger is built on a simple, honest rule: money in is free — Safaricom takes its own cut from the paying customer, and PayChain earns nothing directly on a collection. Money out carries a fee — on every payout (mobile withdrawal, PesaLink, RTGS, KPLC/NCWSC bills, Lipa na M-Pesa B2B), the merchant pays NCBA or Safaricom's real pass-through cost, plus PayChain's own kept margin, shown side by side rather than blended into one opaque number.

It's a fair structure, and it's a real point of difference from a bank account that charges for both directions. But it is a single-lever model — the only way revenue grows is more transfer volume at the same thin per-transaction margin.

Case in point

A recent audit found that Excise Duty — KRA's tax on bank transfer fees, roughly a fifth of NCBA's own charge — had never been priced into PayChain's margin. On the highest-volume rails, that tax alone was quietly erasing most of the kept markup on every transfer. It's now corrected, but it's the clearest illustration of the ceiling on a single-lever model: a cost nobody modeled can silently outrun the only revenue line that exists to absorb it.

The fix going forward isn't just "price it right once" — it's not depending on one thin, cost-sensitive line to carry the whole business.

03

The Proposed Model — Four Pillars

Framework
1
Foundation · Live today

Priced-Right Transfers

The current cost-plus model, kept exactly as is in principle — money in free, money out priced transparently — but now with every real cost, including tax, actually built into the margin rather than discovered later. This stays the floor the other three pillars are built on top of, not something they replace.

Revenue mechanism
Per-transaction service fee, cost-plus, disclosed alongside the real third-party charge.
Merchant sees
Exactly what NCBA/Safaricom charged and exactly what PayChain kept — no blended number.
2
New · Highest revenue potential

Embedded Working Capital

PayChain sees a merchant's real daily cash flow before any bank does — every collection, every payout, months of it. That's exactly the signal used to underwrite fast, fairly-priced short-term capital, without a credit bureau check or collateral. Advance a multiple of a merchant's average weekly collections; recover it automatically as a small, fixed percentage of daily inflows, not a fixed monthly instalment — so a slow week never strains the merchant the way a bank loan repayment would.

Revenue mechanism
A one-time origination fee plus a fixed factor rate on the advance — priced in, not compounding.
Merchant benefit
Same-day capital at a fraction of the effective cost of a typical Kenyan mobile-lending app.
3
New · Requires legal review before launch

Shared Float Yield

Merchant balances sit in a pooled NCBA account between the moment they're received and the moment a merchant withdraws them — money that is, today, simply idle. Placing a portion of consistently-idle float in a short-duration, low-risk instrument and sharing part of the yield back to merchants would be a genuinely rare thing for a Kenyan SME account to offer: a balance that earns something, rather than one that sits flat the way every bank current account does.

Revenue mechanism
A spread between the yield earned and the portion PayChain passes back to merchants.
Before this ships
Needs a legal/compliance read on trust-account rules and PayChain's NCBA agreement — see §08.
4
New · Predictable, recurring

Growth Tier

A monthly subscription for merchants who've outgrown pay-as-you-go: a lower blended transfer fee at volume, more Bulk Pay batch headroom, more team seats, and priority support. It rewards exactly the merchants already doing the most volume — the ones currently paying full price on every single transaction regardless of how much of it they send PayChain's way.

Revenue mechanism
Flat monthly fee, recurring, layered on top of (not instead of) usage revenue.
Merchant benefit
Lower effective cost per transaction as they scale, plus better tooling for a growing team.

07

Implementation Roadmap

Phased
Phase 1 · Now

Foundation

Cost-correct transfer pricing and merchant-facing fee transparency (already underway). Launch the Growth tier using the Bulk Pay and Team infrastructure that already exists — the fastest pillar to ship, since it needs no new underwriting or compliance work.

Phase 2 · Next

Working Capital Pilot

Pilot embedded advances with a small cohort of merchants who already have months of transaction history on the platform. Underwrite conservatively — a fixed multiple of proven average weekly collections, never a guess — and repay automatically as a share of daily inflows.

Phase 3 · Later

Float Yield, If Cleared

Legal and compliance review of float-yield sharing runs in parallel with Phase 2. If it clears, pilot with a small merchant set before any wider rollout. Expand working-capital eligibility as underwriting data accumulates.

08

Risk & Compliance

Read before building
Responsible underwriting

Advance limits must be tied to a merchant's proven cash flow, not an aspirational one — over-extending a thin-margin merchant is the one outcome that breaks the trust this whole model depends on.

Float-yield legality

Requires a proper legal read on trust-account rules, PayChain's NCBA agreement, and whatever licensing posture applies before a single shilling of merchant float is placed anywhere.

Subscription unit economics

Growth-tier pricing above is a starting point, not a locked number — validate against real support cost and the actual margin given up per discounted transaction before publishing a rate card.

Free money-in is not negotiable

It's core to the value proposition and a real point of difference from a bank account. No pillar in this report should ever be allowed to erode it.

09

Recommendation

Closing

Ship Phase 1 first — it requires no new risk, no new compliance work, and rewards PayChain's best merchants immediately. Every pillar after that should earn its place the same way: revenue that grows because merchants are growing, never revenue that grows by charging the same merchant more for the same transaction. That's the version of this business that keeps compounding — merchants stay because the fees are fair, and the fees can afford to stay fair because the business no longer depends on only one of them.
